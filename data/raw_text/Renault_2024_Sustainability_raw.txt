Introduction

The  present  chapter  has  been  completely  redesigned  this  year,  to  comply  with  European  Union’s  CSRD  (Corporate
Sustainability  Reporting  Directive  -  2022/2464/EU)  and  the  French  due  diligence  law  (law  2017-399  related  to  the  Devoir  de
Vigilance).

Both  reglementations  are  based  on  a  risk-driven  approach,  with  some  methodological  differences:  for  example,  potential
negative consequences on environment or society are called “risks“ by the Devoir de Vigilence and assessed on a net basis,
whilst  CSRD  calls  them  “impacts“  and  requires  to  assess  them  on  a  gross  basis.  They  are  nevertheless  consistent  with  the
approach  adopted  by  the  Group’s  major  risk  management  system,  detailed  in  chapter  4  of  this  document.  Cross-references
have been established whenever applicable to avoid redundancy in statements.

Most  of  the  information  of  this  chapter  cover  the  CSRD  requirements.  Being  an  industrial  company  that  designs  and
manufactures complex products and provides mobility and financing services for millions of customers all around the world,
Renault Group has strong interdependencies with the planet and the people that live in. This has led to retain 69 impacts, risks
and  opportunities  as  material,  according  to  the  CSRD  analysis  methodology,  on  each  of  which  Renault  Group’s  governance,
policies, targets and action plans are exposed, following the order of the European Sustainability Reporting standards (ESRS)
and indexed according to EFRAG’s data point list. As required by CSRD, European Taxonomy disclosure is embedded in this part
(See 2.2.2.6).

We also disclose, at the end of this chapter (See 2.4), information related to previously disclosed frameworks (SASB), with cross-
reference to CSRD and/or Devoir de Vigilance data points whenever applicable.

To  help  the  reader  in  catching  the  essentials  of  Renault  Group’s  sustainable  development,  an  executive  summary  of  this
comprehensive chapter is provided below.

Following  the  Paris  Climate  Agreement  (COP  21)  in  2015,
Renault  Group  has  adapted  its  strategy  to  contribute  to
limiting global warming to well below 2°C, and even aims to
continue  efforts  to  limit  the  temperature  rise  to  1.5°C.
Previously,  the  Group  reduced  its  carbon  footprint  intensity
in the automotive sector by 28% from 2010 to 20232.

These  efforts  will  continue  until  2050,  when  the  Group  has
set  a  net  zero  target  to  contribute  to  the  carbon  neutrality
objective, with an interim target to achieve this ambition for
activities  in  Europe  by  2040.  This  will  be  accomplished
through  a  combination  of  90%  emission  reductions  and
offsetting actions for the remaining 10% of emissions based
on the 2019 base year. [DP E1-1_01; DP E1-1_02]

Since 2010, Renault Group has been carrying out greenhouse
gas  estimation  and  life  cycle  assessments  to  evaluate  the
global  warming  potential  of  the  products  it  sells  (See
chapter 2.2.1.2.1). The global warming potential of an internal
combustion engine (ICE) vehicle shows that more than 80%
of  the  greenhouse  gases  are  emitted  during  the  use  phase
(Scope 3 Cat. 11), approximately 17% during the production of
the vehicle components (Scope 3 Cat. 1) including end-of-life,
and 1% during the Group’s operations (Scope 1&2) (data from
average  2024  results  of  the  Renault  Group’s  greenhouse
gas emissions in intensity per vehicle).

Physical risk analysis: Renault Group sites

According  to  this  analysis,  20  sites  of  Renault  Group  are
exposed  to  a  high  risk  of  riverine  and  surface  flooding,
4  sites  are  located  in  regions  with  extreme  water  stress
where  water  consumption  exceeds  availability,  and  6  sites
are  exposed  to  extreme  heat  conditions  that  could
potentially  endanger  employees’  health  and  wellbeing.
Major impacts include business interruption due to extreme
climate  events  and  increase  of  operational  expenditures
(OpEx)  to  control  temperature  in  workshops  and  assembly
lines,  storage  warehouses.  To  address  these  challenges,
Renault  Group  plans  to  build  an  adaptation  and  resilience
action  plan,  based  on  workshops,  climate  adaptation  field
visits,  sustainable  lean  best  practices,  and  adaptation  to
climate  change  ISO  14090  standard  compliance.  Renault
Group will also assess the costs and benefits of each action
with  local  teams  at  priority  sites,  evaluate  the  greenhouse
gas  (GHG)  emissions  associated  with  each  action  and
prioritize their implementation. [E1.IRO-1-04]

2.2.2.1.5.1 Action plan on Renault Group sites climate 

change adaptation

To  control  the  physical  risks  associated  with  extreme  heat,
existing prevention and protection measures allow short-term
control.  These  include  technical  measures  (ventilation,  space
cooling,  storage  conditions  for  batteries  of  electric  vehicles
(EV) or rechargeable hybrid electric vehicles (PHEV), chemical
products,  etc.)  and  organizational  measures  (implementation
of the heat wave plan, work schedules, breaks, etc.). 

The  HSEE  Department  in  coordination  with  other  expert
functions  leads  all  the  sites  concerned  on  these  issues.
A steering committee is organized 4 times a year to monitor
progress and decide on complementary actions. This risk is
the subject of cross-functional governance in the company,
in  a  multi-business  team  (engineering,  purchasing  and
supply  chain  functions)  to  have  a  holistic  view  and  build
shared resilience industrial strategies.

The next step is to identify and calibrate threshold values for all
our  sites  in  2025  by  integrating  the  main  physical  factors
ambient temperature, humidity, activity process characteristics
and then develop an adaptation plan for the medium term.

To control the physical risks related to water stress, Renault
Group  has  implemented  since  2023  a  policy  dedicated  to
water  management  known  as  «Full  Power  Water»  on  all
industrial sites, detailed in chapter 2.2.2.3.2.

To  control  the  physical  risks  associated  with  flooding,
Renault  Group  has  previously  carried  out  actions  on
industrial  sites.  It  undertakes  the  necessary  maintenance
actions in order to maintain the efficiency of these facilities.
The  approach  has  been  extended  to  rainwater  drainage
systems since late 2024.

2.2.2.1.5.2 Action plan on climate change mitigation

Renault Group’s strategic actions, within its decarbonization
framework,  embrace  all  three  stages  of  its  value  chain.  An
internal  carbon  target  is  set  for  each  new  vehicle  project
in  the  upstream  design  phase.  Each  area  of  expertise
involved in design – vehicle aerodynamics, weight, choice of
materials,  battery  size,  etc.  –  identifies  and  leverages  the
solutions available for reducing its carbon impact.

A. Raw material procurement

Over  the  entire  life  cycle  of  an  internal  combustion  engine

doubles  the  production’s  emissions  when  usage  emissions
drop.  (See  graphs  in  chapter  2.2.2.1.1.)  Renault  Group  has  laid
out  internal  commitments  to  curb  the  carbon  impact  of

production of Renault Group’s vehicles.

logistics activities reporting on the type of transport used, kilometers traveled, volumes of vehicles 
transported and the filling rate of containers. Emission factors used are obtained from the Clean 
Cargo Working Group for sea transport and from ADEME  for other types of transport.

Daily commuting

Emissions from employee business travel (train, plane) are calculated based on the reporting from 
the travel agency, which integrates the number of kilometers traveled, the type of transport, type of 
service (economy, business, etc.) and uses emission factors published by the Department for 
Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (DEFRA) of the United Kingdom, which are updated annually.

Emissions from vehicles and public transport for employee commuting are calculated from 
information provided by employees in the Renault Group personnel database. Employees provide 
information on the type of transport used (private vehicle or public transport) and the home-work 
distance. To calculate emissions generated, the Group uses average emission factors from ADEME. 
Emissions prevented through homeworking are deducted. This indicator is calculated for the daily 
commuting of Renault Group France employees and extrapolated worldwide.

bodywork assembly plant manufacturing electric vehicle. The second site is Cléon, a 
Castings & Mechanical plant manufacturing engines and gearboxes mostly for electric

manufactured by Douai is applied to the total number of vehicles produced by the group.

● For Research & Development activities, the volume of substances is not significant with 

regard to manufacturing and is excluded from the perimeter. 

To improve the reliability and accuracy of the communicated data, Renault Group has 
initiated an overhaul of its chemical management system, which will be implemented by 
the end of 2025 and will enable the enlargement of the perimeter of the sites covered. 

The volumes of chemical products purchased are based on actual data as of the end of 
November, and the annual quantities of substances are extrapolated on this basis. 

Substances of very high concern that leave facilities as product 

To determine the quantities of substances that leave Renault Group’s sites through the 
produced vehicles, a detailed analysis of the composition of an electric vehicle and a 
thermal vehicle is carried out. For each vehicle, the quantity of substances is determined 
using its technical definition, which provides a detailed breakdown of the parts that 
compose it. The quantities of substances contained in each part are then identified 
based on the “Material Data Sheets“ (MdS). This analysis allows for determining a 
minimum and maximum quantity of substance contained in these vehicles. To 
extrapolate the results to the entire scope, the interval values are multiplied by the 
number of vehicles manufactured during the reporting period.

The same analysis is carried out on 80% of after-sales volumes, corresponding to the 
minimum values of the intervals. Maximum values are extrapolated to the entire 
perimeter by proportionality.

To improve the reliability and accuracy of the communicated data, Renault Group is 
working on software developments to automate data collection. This will allow Renault 
Group to consider more vehicles to adjust the accuracy and reduce the range of the values.

Substances of very high concern that leave facilities as service 

To determine the volume of substances that leave the facilities as services, an analysis of 
chemical products sold by the after-sales division has been conducted (e.g., windshield washer 
fluid, lubricant, polish, etc.). The list of products for which at least one substance of very high 
concern is declared under Article 57 of the REACH regulation is established. Based on this 
information and the quantities sold, the amounts of substances have been determined.

New vehicles: 

For new vehicles, the weight of recycled or reused materials is determined using “pivot“ vehicles, which 
represent groups of vehicles with common characteristics. Each vehicle is associated to a pivot, and material 
content is derived from the Bill of Materials (BOM). The proportion of recycled materials is based on circular 
economy data. If no pivot exists, a minimum recycled material rate is applied based on the engine type.

For waste, the scope is limited waste from the manufacture of new vehicles, as other types of waste are 
considered within their respective scopes. To determine the weight of recycled or reused materials, data on 
waste generated is collected by material type, and the proportion of recycled or reused materials is 
determined based on circular economy data. 

After-sale: 

For after-sales, the following methodologies have been used: 

● Parts remanufactured by Renault Group: to determine the weight of reused material during the

Renault Group’s main supplier and relates to the following five categories of parts: starter, alternator,
